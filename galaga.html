RIGO'S SPACE FORCE

Earth's last lone fighter squadron against the Tesla fleet of the evil Space X — a sister company of Skynet. (Yes, that Skynet. The cars learned to fly, and they brought friends.)

The invasion

You fly for Rigo's Space Force — one fighter, then (if you're good) two, holding the line above Earth. Coming down at you is the Tesla fleet: forty all-electric warships sent by the evil Space X, a sister company of Skynet — same family business of machines deciding humanity is a bug to be patched, now with better batteries and a charging network. 😉

The fleet flies in three classes: 20 Tesla Drones low, 16 Tesla Cruisers in the middle, and 4 Tesla Motherships on top — the big two-hit captors with the tractor beam. Sleek silver hulls, red accents, zero emissions, total menace. Send every last one to the scrapyard.

How to play

  • ← → or A/D — move (left–right only, like the cabinet)
  • Space / Z — fire. Max 2 shots on screen per Fighter — the limit is the fire rate.
  • F or the Auto-Fire button — toggle Auto-Fire. When it's ON, your Space Force fighter fires continuously at the game's max rate (still capped at 2 shots on screen per Fighter, 4 for the Dual Fighter) — no holding the fire button. Default is OFF; it stays as you set it for the whole session, even across games.
  • On touch screens: drag on the cabinet, or use the ◀ FIRE ▶ pads. The Auto-Fire button above works by tap, too.
  • P — pause. Sound starts on your first tap/key (browser rule).

The capture gambit

A Tesla Mothership sometimes stops mid-screen and pours out its shimmering blue tractor beam. Get caught and that Fighter is gone — it costs a life, and the red captive flies beside its captor Mothership. Shoot the captor down while it dives with the captive in tow and the Fighter breaks free, docks beside yours, and you fly the Dual Fighter: two shots per volley, up to 4 on screen — but twice as wide, and a hit costs only the half that was struck.

Kill the captor Mothership in formation instead and there's no rescue — the captive dives off alone and comes back next stage, bound to a new Mothership. And never shoot the captive itself: it's 500/1,000 points for a ship you'll never get back.

Challenging Stages

Stages 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23, 27, 31, then the set repeats: 40 Teslas in 5 groups of 8 stream past on looping paths. They don't shoot and don't dive at you — it's a shooting gallery. 100 points a hit, a bonus per complete group of 8 wiped out (1,000–3,000 by stage), and 10,000 for a Perfect 40.

Honest gaps

This is a re-skin with new fiction on top: underneath, it's the original game. Namco never published exact pixel speeds for the Fighter, bullets, bombs or dives, the per-stage bomb caps, or the flight-path data — those are tuned by feel at 60 Hz here, and the entry/dive/challenge choreography is rebuilt from the documented pattern archetypes (side processions, double rows, long strings), not extracted from the ROM. Everything else on this page — counts, scores, capture rules, the Challenging cadence — is the original Galaga spec, documented in the sources below. Transform trios appear from Stage 4 in the documented rotation (Scorpions → Bosconian Ei → Galaxian Flagships), flying here as experimental Tesla prototypes.

Galaxian (1979), for the record

The original game's predecessor differs where it counts: 46 aliens appearing instantly in 6 rows (2 Flagships, 6 Escorts, 8 Emissaries, 30 Drones — one source counts 44 + 2 Flagships), a formation that simply marches side-to-side with no pulse, 1 shot on screen, 1-hit Flagships, no capture, no Dual Fighter, no Challenging Stages — and a Flagship's 800-point trick that depends on killing its escorts first. This build follows Galaga's rules — with Teslas in the alien seats.
