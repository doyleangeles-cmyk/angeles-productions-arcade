The Angeles family edition — pass-and-play with friends, plus AI at any seat. Original board drawn in code; classic Atlantic City streets, prices & rents.

Players — 2 to 4 seats

Each seat: Human (hot-seat), AI, or Off. You need at least two seats in the game. Tokens can't repeat.

House rule taxes, fines & fees feed the center pot; land on Free Parking to collect it

How a turn works

Roll the dice, move, then buy the property — or it goes to auction. Doubles roll again; three doubles in a row sends you to Jail. Build houses on a full color set, mortgage when you're short, trade with anyone. Last player standing wins. Full rules and the rent tables are below the board once the game starts.

How to play

  • Goal: be the last player who isn't bankrupt. Everyone starts with $1,500 and collects $200 each time they pass GO.
  • Your turn: roll two dice and move. Doubles roll again — but three doubles in a row sends you straight to Jail. When your turn ends, the device passes to the next human (AI seats play themselves).
  • Buying: land on an unowned property and you may buy it at the printed price. Pass on it — or can't afford it — and it goes to auction: everyone bids, highest bid wins it at that price.
  • Rent: landing on someone else's property pays rent automatically. Owning a full color set doubles rent on unimproved lots and lets you build. Railroads pay $25 / $50 / $100 / $200 for 1–4 owned. Utilities charge 4× the dice roll, or 10× if both are owned.
  • Houses & hotels: build evenly across a set — no lot may be more than one house ahead of its partners. Four houses swap for a hotel. House price depends on the row: $50, $100, $150, or $200. Selling returns half price.
  • Jail: get out by paying $50 before you roll, rolling doubles within three turns (a third failure costs the $50 and you move the roll), or playing a Get Out of Jail Free card. While in Jail you still collect rent.
  • Mortgaging: flip a deed to the bank for half its price (all houses on that color set must be sold first). No rent is collected on a mortgaged deed. Lift the mortgage for the mortgage value plus 10%.
  • Trading: on your turn, offer any player cash, deeds, and jail cards for theirs. A mortgaged deed that changes hands costs the new owner a 10% transfer fee — pay it now, or owe it on top of the unmortgage cost later.
  • Taxes & Free Parking (house rule, on by default): Income Tax is a flat $200, Luxury Tax $100. With the Free Parking jackpot on, every tax, fine and fee paid to the bank — taxes, jail fines, card payments, repair bills — goes into the pot in the middle of the board (rent and purchase prices don't). Land on Free Parking and you collect the whole pot. Turn the jackpot off on the setup screen to play the official rule instead, where Free Parking is just a rest space and those payments vanish to the bank.
  • Bankruptcy: if you owe more than you can raise by selling houses and mortgaging, everything you have goes to whoever you owed (or back to the bank) and you're out.

Rent tables

Rent with a full color set and no houses is double the base rent. All figures in dollars.

Chance & Community Chest

Each deck holds 16 cards in our own words with the classic effects: advances to GO, Boardwalk, Illinois Ave, St. Charles Place, Reading Railroad, the nearest railroad (double rent) or nearest utility (10× a fresh roll), go back 3 spaces, go to Jail, Get Out of Jail Free, house/hotel repair bills, chairmen paying every player, birthdays collecting from every player, bank dividends, inheritances, fines and fees. Drawn cards cycle to the bottom of the deck; a jail card stays in your hand until you use it.

Notes

  • The bank holds 32 houses and 12 hotels. If houses run out, you simply can't build more until some come back (no building auctions).
  • AI players buy with a cash reserve, bid up to what a property is worth to them, build on their monopolies, and judge trades by whether the deal improves their position.
  • Board art, token glyphs, and card wording are original to this page; street names and the price/rent numbers are the standard published game data.
