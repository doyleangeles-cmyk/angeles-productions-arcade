BurgerTime — order up!

You are chef Peter Pepper. Walk clear across each giant ingredient and it drops a level — knock the whole stack down onto the plates to build every burger on the board, while Mr. Hot Dog, Mr. Egg and (from Stage 3) Mr. Pickle hunt you through the planks and ladders. Your only weapon: a shaker of pepper, 5 shots to start.

How to play

  • ←→ walk · ↑↓ climb · Space pepper · P pause. (WASD works too.)
  • Walk the full length of an ingredient — every section you cross dips down. Cross them all and the whole layer falls one level.
  • Ladders are exact: line up with the ladder's centre to get on, and you can only step off at the very top or bottom. One step too high or low and you stay on. That's the original's rule — and its #1 killer.
  • You can stop and wait mid-ladder — a classic way to lure the pack onto a floor below you.
  • No jump. There is no jump. Peter Pepper has never jumped.

The chasers

Mr. Hot Dog — crush 100 ptsThe most numerous. First board: 3 of him, plus one Mr. Egg.
Mr. Egg — crush 300 ptsA walking fried egg — eggs on burgers are common in Japan, hence his existence.
Mr. Pickle — crush 200 ptsDoesn't show up until Stage 3, when the crew grows to six.

All three use the same platforms, ladders and chase logic in the 1982 arcade game — claims that each has its own distinct AI pattern come from the 2019 sequel BurgerTime Party!, not the original. They can't reverse instantly, and a freshly appeared enemy can be passed through for about ¼ second.

Big money moves

  • Let them ride: wait on the last section of an ingredient until enemies climb on, then finish the crossing. A falling ingredient drops 2 extra levels per rider — and pays 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 / 4,000 / 8,000 / 16,000 for 1–6 riders.
  • Work top-down: a falling layer knocks the layer below it down too — one good drop can cascade a whole burger.
  • Herd the pack: duck slightly up or down a ladder and the chasers take the first ladder that reaches you — bunch them up, then pepper or drop them all at once.
  • Bonus food appears near the centre of the maze after enough ingredients reach the bottom (Stage 1: after 4, 8 and 12). Grab it for points and +1 pepper.

Faithful bits — and the honest gaps

Built from a study of the 1982 arcade original (Data East, distributed by Bally Midway in the US; designer credited as Yuji Gondo; Japan Aug 25 1982 as Hamburger, North America Nov 1982). Where the record is solid, this game follows it exactly. Where it isn't, the gap is printed here rather than papered over.

Straight from the record

  • Segmented dips: each ingredient is divided into sections; every section you pass sags a bit and stays sagged, and only a full crossing drops the layer — one pass, no hit-points. Half-trampled ingredients flatten again if Peter dies (reported by a player of the arcade, via a faithful remake's comments).
  • Chain falls: a layer landing on the layer below knocks that one down a level too, staggered in flight rather than teleporting as a column.
  • Riding falls: an ingredient carrying enemies falls farther — the arcade/Wikipedia formula used here is 1 + 2n levels for n riders, capped at the plate. Riders sit out the fall and aren't dangerous again until they step off.
  • Pepper: 5 shots at start, thrown as a short cloud in the direction Peter faces; every enemy touching it is stunned and can be walked through. One puff can stun the whole bunched pack.
  • Enemies are never gone: crush one and another appears at a platform edge; dropped riders resume from where they landed.
  • Six boards, then it loops with faster, more accurate pursuers — and enemies also speed up the longer a single board drags on. Peter's speed never changes, ever.
  • The mercy bug: die at the exact moment the final ingredient completes the last burger and the game does not take the life. It's in here.
  • No spawn protection for Peter is documented in the 1982 game — so none is given. (The ~10-second invulnerable respawn belongs to the 2019 sequel.)
  • Stage 28 anomaly (documented, deep in the loop): from Stage 28 enemies move about 2½× faster — then, after 90 seconds on one chef, slow to one step every 5 seconds.

Open questions

  • Exact speeds were never published — no px/second figures exist for Peter or the enemies at any difficulty. The speeds in this build are tuned to the documented behaviour (Peter constant; enemies faster per board, per cycle, and over time on a board), not copied numbers.
  • Pepper stun duration: every source says only "a few seconds." This build uses ~2.5 s as a design choice.
  • Dip segments per ingredient: sources say only "several sections." This build uses 4.
  • Riding-fall formula conflict: the Intellivision manual (a port) says a ride drops 3 levels with one enemy, +1 per extra enemy (n + 2). This build uses the arcade/Wikipedia 1 + 2n.
  • Bonus-trigger off-by-one: two MAME-info sources give Stage 3's last trigger as 13 and Stage 4's as 29; a faithful-remake thread says 14 and 28. This build uses the MAME-info table (L1 4/8/12 · L2 5/7/13 · L3 4/8/13 · L4 6/16/22/29 · L5 3/6/9 · L6 3/7/12/14).
  • Boards 2–6 exact tile maps were not published in text sources — only their documented character (Stage 3: 6 burgers of 3 layers and the first Mr. Pickle; Stage 4: 4 burgers of 8 layers with staircase ladders; Stage 5: 2 burgers, 6 enemies, few routes; Stage 6: side burgers on dead ends). Boards here follow those counts and characters; their precise plank-and-ladder maps are this build's arrangement, and Stage 1 follows its documented stack exactly: Top Bun – Lettuce – Patty – Bottom Bun, four burgers, layers at most five floors from the plate.
  • Palette size conflict: 16 colours in the Bally Midway hardware listing vs. 32 in the DECO Cassette listing — likely a real hardware-revision difference, unresolved in the sources. Either way: flat, saturated arcade colour, no gradients.
  • Exact respawn positions for Peter and reset enemies after a death are not published; this build restarts Peter at centre with enemies reset to the edges.
  • Music: the original loops a short, cheerful AY-chip tune with distinct jingles for start, drops, pepper, squash, bonus, level complete and death. The exact loop length is not published (the "~5-second loop" figure describes the NES port). The tune here is an original square-wave loop in that style, not the arcade melody.
